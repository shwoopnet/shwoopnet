## 1. Can the registered trade be entered?
Registered: at 15:50 ET, on seeing an imbalance above 5% of 20-day ADV, take the other side by limit-on-close. NYSE: ordinary MOC/LOC entry and cancel stop at 15:50 exactly when the imbalance first publishes; after that only orders that "offset the significant imbalance" may be entered, and cancels are rejected. So a participant who reacts to the 15:50 message CANNOT place an ordinary LOC; the only entry is an offsetting order (Closing Offset type). Nasdaq: the entry cutoff was not found; imbalance-only orders exist but their cutoff and who may use them are unread. Not shown: whether a retail-size account can send an offsetting or imbalance-only order through any broker. The trade as registered is therefore NOT enterable as written (limit-on-close at 15:50 on NYSE); it is enterable only as an offsetting order, a different order type with no cancel and execution only at the auction price.

## 2. Re-register or retire?
RE-REGISTER before any data (new text, not an edit of the old rule): "At the NYSE or Nasdaq published imbalance of at least 5% of 20-day ADV, enter an offsetting order (closing offset or imbalance-only) on the other side, executed at the official closing price if filled; exit at the next official open." Effects on the edge: (a) the entry price is the closing cross price, not a price we choose, so any imbalance-driven close dislocation is the thing earned, and the price impact the other side pays is the gross edge; (b) fill is uncertain: a small order is filled only if it offsets, so the sample is conditioned on fills the data cannot show (the imbalance feed does not show our fill), a selection issue the test would have to treat as an assumption (fill at the cross price if our side is the offsetting side and the printed imbalance is larger than our size); (c) no cancel, so no risk control before the print; (d) exit at the next open adds an overnight gap risk of about 60 to 100 bp sd per name, which swamps a 10 to 30 bp edge per event and needs many events to see; the card should also report the close-to-5-minutes-later result as the cleaner measure of the forced flow. If the Head wants the old text kept, retire S8 instead: its registered entry is impossible on NYSE. My recommendation: re-register as above, LEAD-only, and ask the owner for nothing until the pilot proves the price definition.

## 3. Who is on the other side, and why would a small order fill
Competitors for the offsetting side: designated market makers and large proprietary and index-arbitrage desks with direct exchange access and the right to submit offsetting orders in size, plus exchange-run auction mechanisms that quote against the imbalance (price improvement paid to the offsetting side). The imbalance exists because index funds, ETFs and passive baskets must trade at the close regardless of price; those participants are the ones who lose, and the desks above are already paid for it. A small order would fill because the auction clears at one price and offsetting orders ahead of it are filled first by price priority; a few hundred shares on the offsetting side is a small part of an imbalance in the tens of thousands of shares, so it fills if its limit is at or better than the clearing price. We would not be competing on size but on being in the queue at all, and the real question (see the Head's rulings) is whether we can reach that order type at a broker. I do not know, and no public document I read answers it. Realistic ceiling: a LEAD at n of 150 to 300 events, and a costed request to the owner for more history.
