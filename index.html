The method behind the screener, and a real backtest — not a demo.
The Method

The daily screener isn't a black box. Here's the whole thing, in plain language — including what it can't tell you yet.

1
A fixed list of 41 stocks, not the whole market
Mixed across sectors and company sizes — chips, retail, industrials, energy, healthcare, banks — not just the same handful of giant names. It only ever picks from this list.
2
The trend has to actually agree with the trade
The 20-day and 50-day averages have to line up with the direction of the trade. A stock that just had a big price swing but has no real trend gets skipped — a wild week isn't enough on its own.
3
Entry, stop, and target come from the actual chart
Entry is the 20-day average. Stop is just past the actual 20-day low or high. Target is the real 20-day resistance or support — not some fixed multiple of the risk, which would just make every trade's ratio the same number no matter what.
4
Ranked by momentum, top 4 make the list
Everything that passes the trend check gets ranked by how much it's moved in the last 5 days. Top 4 become the day's picks. If nothing passes on a given day, fewer than 4 show up — that's fine, better than forcing a bad pick.
What this isn't (yet)

This scoring is a heuristic I came up with — not something proven against real results yet. There's a log quietly tracking what actually happens to every pick: hit, stopped, or never resolved. It's building toward real numbers instead of a guess, but there isn't enough history yet to trust a win rate. The backtest below is the honest next step: same logic, run against real history, so you can see how it actually would have done instead of just taking my word for it.

Backtest

Runs the same scoring and stop/target logic used live, day by day, against real historical prices — only ever using data that would have actually existed at that point in time, no peeking ahead. One symbol at a time, not the full 41-stock list (testing "would this have been today's pick" across the whole list is a much bigger job — this checks whether the underlying signal itself actually works).
