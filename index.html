What Shwoop actually does

It's a robot trader. It watches a list of 16 stocks, waits for one to make a strong, real move early in the trading day, then buys or sells it automatically — picks how much to risk, places the order, and closes the trade out. You don't have to do anything.

What "profit factor" means: add up every dollar it's won, add up every dollar it's lost, and divide the two. It's the simplest way to answer "does this actually make money" — not just how often it wins.
Out of 4,317 real trades tested, risking $100 on each one (1% of a $10,000 account) — 1,736 wins, 2,581 losses:
Total won ($132,286.29) ÷ total lost ($53,408.81) = 2.48
Above 1 means it makes money overall. Below 1 means it loses money overall — no matter how often it "wins." 2.48 means: for every $1 it's lost, it's made back about $2.48.

Tested against over a year of real market data, including real trading fees — not a best-case fantasy number. Exits use a trailing stop, not a fixed target, so a real winner can run further than a flat cap would allow. Dollar figures assume a flat $100 risked per trade, not compounded, since many of these trades happen on the same day across different stocks rather than one after another.

The Method

How the intraday screener actually works, in plain language.

1
16 stocks, chosen for how they're priced, not their size
PLTR, SOFI, RIVN, COIN, MARA, AFRM, DKNG, CVNA, HOOD, RIOT, UPST, LCID, OPEN, SNAP, CHPT, ROKU — volatile, retail-attention-heavy names that trade less efficiently than mega-cap tech. (An earlier universe of large-cap names like AAPL and MSFT was tested extensively and retired — it sat close to breakeven after realistic trading costs, no matter how it was refined.)
2
The first 30 minutes set the range
The high and low of a stock's first two 15-minute bars each day — the opening range. Everything else is judged against whether price actually breaks out of it.
3
The breakout has to happen on real volume, not just a price move
A break beyond the opening range only counts if that bar's volume is at least 2x its own preceding 20-bar average. This single requirement is what separates a genuinely validated edge from one that wasn't — the same breakout logic without this volume check performed meaningfully worse in backtesting.
4
Entry and stop come straight from the range — the exit now trails
Entry is the range boundary itself; the initial stop is the opposite side of the range. Once a trade moves 0.75x its own risk in its favor, the stop starts trailing behind the best price reached instead of staying fixed at a flat 1.5x target — a real winner can run further than a hard cap would allow. One signal per stock per day — once a stock breaks out and qualifies, it doesn't fire again until the next trading day.
What's actually live right now

This is the strategy that replaced the prior SMA-crossover approach, backed by real validation: every one of the 16 universe symbols individually cleared a profit factor above 1.5 across a full year-plus of historical data. The exit was upgraded from a fixed 1.5x target to a trailing stop (activating at 0.75x risk) after walk-forward testing showed it improves both the overall profit factor (2.15 → 2.32) and the worst-case result across 5 independent, non-overlapping time windows (2.03 → 2.15) together — with realistic trading costs included throughout, not a frictionless best case. It's still genuinely new, though — the activity log and the numbers building up there over time are what will show whether real, ongoing performance holds up the way the backtest suggests it should.

Backtest

Runs the same scoring and stop/target logic used live, on 15-minute bars, against real historical prices — only ever using data that would have existed at that point in time. Measures raw % return per trade, independent of account size, so sizing effects don't blur whether the underlying edge is real. If a longer lookback fails to load, try a shorter one — the free data tier's depth isn't fully known.
