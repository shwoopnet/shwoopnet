## 1. What does not change

1. The research bar (cash profit factor 1.5 on development and held-out, three windows, after fees, deflated) and the proven bar (1.3 or better over 100 fills in 2 of 3 windows) stay. They are what any size above the wheel's fixed size needs. [design]
2. The declined list (lifecycle 7e), the no-hedge direction lock, the data-source rules and the shared 12 percent loss stop, 5.5 percent window cap and second-slot caps apply to wheel orders as to any slot order. The wheel never re-admits a row the owner declined.
3. Gold L1 is not a wheel strategy. It keeps its own kill latch and return rule (`docs/GOLD_KILL_LATCH.md`).
4. **The owner's popup is still required for every strategy, once, at admission:** each rule joins the wheel only by the owner's popup with its exact spec (market, side, band, look window, limit, exit, size, kill lines), then the Chief of Staff's reviewed merge and security pass. After that the rule takes and leaves a seat by itself. In code that means one reviewed registry entry per rule in the second slot (and, for a rule that holds to settlement, one reviewed entry on the hold list), written at admission and armed or disarmed only by the replay. Nothing stored, requested or typed on a page may add, enlarge or re-arm a strategy; the in or out state is replayed from the rule's own rows on every run, like the gold latch.
5. **Favourites at 88c or above ride the wheel only under the owner's rule of section 0.8** (a record of at least 150 signals holding at least 10 losses; the arithmetic is in section 4). A row on the declined list stays declined whatever its price.

## 2. The wheel

1. A shadow list of 20 to 30 registered rules as the target. **No maximum on live seats; at least 5 is the target** (the owner, section 0).
2. A rule is in exactly one state: SHADOW (logging, no money), CONFIRMING, QUALIFIED (waiting for a seat), LIVE (a seat, 10 contracts), COOLING (out, 14 days), RETIRED.
3. **One place per market (the owner, section 0.6).** A rule that runs on several markets holds a separate shadow place, record, seat and kill on each, for example the price-jump rule on five sports. Each market version is admitted with its own spec line. Outside the wheel the Nov 11 rule is unchanged: the same logic on a new market is a version of its parent, not a new strategy, and is never counted as a new unique idea in research.

## 3. Admission to the shadow list

1. A pre-registration written before the rule's first counted signal: entry, side, price band, look window, limit, exit, size, its classification card and the declined-list check.
2. **Forward signals only.** A signal counts only if it happened after the registration was committed. A backtest or a development read can nominate a rule. It can never qualify one.
3. **The admit list is fixed before its window.** Rules are admitted in dated batches; nobody adds a rule because its recent past looks good, and nobody picks the best of several variants after seeing them. One set of parameters per rule, the same on every market it runs on; a changed parameter is a new rule, its count restarts, and it is counted again in the lane ledger. Markets differ, parameters do not: nobody tunes a band or a look time market by market.
4. A rule with negative evidence (a falsified read at about 100 trades or more) is not admitted.

## 4. The record and the entry bar [design]

**The record.** Logged by the bot's shadow logger on the quotes of the moment, or replayed from recorded order books. Never market-list quotes and never candles alone. A signal is **fillable** only if a second look within 4 seconds after the decision (one extra public read a row; the Chief of Staff confirms it is buildable inside the read budget) still shows the wheel's size at or under the registered limit (the touch plus at most 1c); no second look is no trade. **Variant for slower logs (the Head of R&D's ruling, Nov 13, 2026 00:07Z, found by Forensics; reported to the owner in the next six-hour line; it does not loosen the rule):** on a log that polls slower than every 4 seconds (NC15 polls every 5), the second look is the NEXT poll and it must come at most 6 seconds after the signal poll; a signal whose next poll comes later is recorded as "not fill-checked" and does not count toward a block. A later look is the stricter test for an immediate order. On R6 (2 second polls) and the wheel book recorder (3 second polls) the rule stays 4 seconds as written. Measured on recorded gold books, an immediate order at the price seen filled about half the time a few seconds later, so a shadow that assumes a fill at the touch overstates its count by a quarter to a half. Cash basis, fee rounded up to the cent for 10 contracts.

**The bar, read once at the end of a block:**

1. A block is 60 fillable signals in a row, within the last 21 days, on at least 7 different days. (Until Nov 13, 2026 this line ended 'no single day more than 30 percent of the block's net'; the owner dropped that limit, section 0.13.)
2. Cash profit factor 1.3 or better on the block and 1.0 or better in each half.
3. At least 10 of the rarer outcome in the block (wins for a cheap-side rule, losses for a rule that buys above 50c); otherwise the block keeps growing until it has them. **For a rule that buys at 88c or above (the owner, section 0.8): the block is at least 150 signals and runs on until it holds at least 10 losses.** On the coin flips a no-edge favourite clears that about 9 to 10 percent of the time, and about 5 percent with the confirmation, which puts it level with the other prices instead of the 27 percent of the bare rule.
4. Blocks never overlap. After a failed block the next one starts from new signals. No rolling re-reads.
5. **Confirmation (decided by the owner):** the next 30 fillable signals after a cleared block hold 1.0 or better. Then the rule is QUALIFIED.
6. **Seats:** a qualified rule takes a seat at once; there is no maximum number of seats. (If the owner ever sets one, the rule with the longest record goes first, never the one with the highest number: picking the highest number picks the luckiest.)
7. A rule that would buy the same market, side and minute as a live strategy does not take a seat while that strategy is live.

**How a block is counted, where the seven lines above leave a choice (the Head of R&D's rulings, Nov 13, 2026 02:05Z, on four gaps Forensics found while writing the second scorer, before any real row was opened by either builder; reported to the owner in the next six-hour line; each picks the stricter or the already frozen reading and none loosens the bar):**

- (a) **Fewer than 7 days.** A block that reaches 60 fillable signals on fewer than 7 different days is not read and is not failed: it keeps growing, signal by signal in order, exactly as line 3 says for the rarer outcome. The block ends, and is read once, at the first signal at which all three counts hold together: at least 60 signals (150 for a rule that buys at 88c or above), at least 7 different days, at least 10 of the rarer outcome. The 7 day count does not depend on any result. The rarer-outcome count does, as line 3 already says. How often a rule clears a block grown this way was recomputed on Nov 13 (the correction note above the table).
- (b) **More than 21 days.** A block must be complete within 21 days, measured from its first signal's time to its last signal's time (21 x 24 hours). A block that is not complete by then ends UNREAD and is recorded as EXPIRED (too slow for the wheel), which is a different record from FAILED: no profit factor is read from it. The next block starts as (k) says; (k) governs. The start of a block never slides forward to drop old signals.
- (c) **The rarer outcome for a rule that may buy either side between 10c and 90c:** at least 10 wins AND at least 10 losses.
- (d) **A day** is a US Central calendar day (America/Chicago, daylight saving followed), taken from the signal poll's estimated exchange time, the clock of (f). The day is used for the 7 day count.
- (e) **Halves** are by signal count in order: the first half is the first floor(n / 2) signals of the block and the second half is the rest.
- (f) **The clock for the second look** is the same clock the frozen sheet uses for the decision time: the estimated exchange time of each poll (the recorder's receive time plus the run's rolling offset from the server's Date header). 'At most 6 seconds' compares those two times, with the 1e-9 tolerance.
- **Second round, 02:09Z: ten further places Auditor B found open on a paper read of the text above, each ruled before either builder went on.**
- (g) **Money is counted in whole cents.** Each signal's cash is in integer cents after the fee rounded up. Where a scorer's cash for a signal is not a whole cent (a fill price averaged over book levels with fractional sizes), it is rounded DOWN to the cent, toward the worse result for the rule, by one expression that every build uses: cents = floor(100 x cash in dollars + 1e-6). A win of $6.754 is 675 cents; a loss of $6.754 is minus 676 cents. (Ruled 02:15Z on Auditor B's question; the frozen scorers are not changed.) Gross wins W and gross losses L are sums of integer cents. '1.3 or better' is 10 x W >= 13 x L; '1.0 or better' is W >= L; no float and no tolerance. A block, half or confirmation with L = 0 and W > 0 passes; W = 0 fails.
- (h) **REMOVED by the owner on Nov 13, 2026 (section 0.13): there is no single-day limit in the bar.** (It read: for every US Central day in the block, 100 x that day's net in cents > 30 x the block's net in cents fails the block.) A block reader applies no day-concentration test.
- (i) **A rule's class comes from its registered price band, fixed at registration, never from its fills.** The price that decides is the signal's limit in whole cents, and a band is read as the range of limits it allows (for the NC15 depth cell, asks of 10c to 90c are limits of 11c to 91c). A band wholly at or under 50c needs 10 wins; wholly above 50c needs 10 losses; a band that crosses 50c needs both. **88c:** a signal whose limit is 88c or above is a favourite under the owner's line (section 0.8). A rule takes such signals only if it is registered in the favourites class as a whole, and then every block of it is at least 150 signals with at least 10 losses (and 10 wins too if its band crosses 50c). A rule not registered in the favourites class takes NO signal with a limit of 88c or above: such a signal is skipped as in (m), and a seat of that rule does not trade it. The NC15 depth-cell places (band 10c to 90c on the ask) are not in the favourites class, so their signals with a limit of 88c or above are skipped. The Head chose this over putting the whole rule in the favourites class; the owner is told both readings in the six-hour line and may choose the other.
- (j) **The confirmation.** Its 30 signals are the next 30 counted signals after the cleared block's last signal. No minimum number of days and no rarer-outcome count. It must be complete within 21 x 24 hours of the cleared block's last signal, else it is EXPIRED and unread, and the rule starts a new block. A confirmation that does not hold W >= L is FAILED. After a confirmation, passed, failed or expired, no signal of it ever counts toward any block; the next block starts at the first counted signal after it.
- (k) **When EXPIRED is decided.** By the clock, not by the next signal, and (q) governs where a result is open: a block is EXPIRED once the estimated exchange time passes its first signal's time plus 21 x 24 hours without it being complete (a confirmation: once it passes the cleared block's last signal's time plus 21 x 24 hours, as (j) says), and the reader, which is always run with a stated 'now', writes that to the record. If the three counts of (a) first hold at a signal later than that, the block is EXPIRED, not read. The next block starts at the first counted signal after the expiry time.
- (l) **Which signals count from the start time.** A signal counts only if its decision (signal poll) time, on the clock of (f), is at or after the start time written as a UTC timestamp in the place's line of WHEEL_ADMIT_LIST.md. A signal decided before it does not count even if its second look falls after.
- (m) **Skipped signals.** A signal that is a miss, is not fill-checked, or is skipped under (i) is not in any block: it does not break 'in a row', and it does not enter the signal count, the day count, the 21 days or the money. 'In a row' means consecutive counted signals in time order, none left out by choice.
- (n) **Open results and voids.** A block or confirmation is read only when every signal in it has a final result. A void is in the count n and its fee is in L, but it is neither a win nor a loss for the rarer-outcome count.
- (o) **Which look on the faster logs.** Not chosen again: the frozen sheet already says it. On R6 (2 second polls) and the wheel book recorder (3 second polls) the second look is the FIRST poll after the decision poll, and it must come at most 4 seconds after it on the clock of (f); a later one is no second look.
- **Third round, 02:15Z: five more places from Auditor B's re-read, and two conventions the builders asked about.**
- (p) **Order of signals.** Counted signals are ordered by decision time on the clock of (f), then by market ticker ascending, then by side (yes before no). The 60th or 150th signal, the halves and the first completion of (a) all use this order.
- (q) **Complete, and EXPIRED, when results are still open.** A block is complete if the counts of (a) hold on signals whose decision time is at most its first signal's time plus 21 x 24 hours, with the results known when the reader runs. The reader writes EXPIRED only when every counted signal up to that time has a final result and the counts still do not hold; until then it writes 'waiting for results, expiry not yet decided'. So the same data read earlier and later can never give EXPIRED and then not EXPIRED. The same holds for a confirmation and its own 21 x 24 hours under (j).
- (r) **First completion across an open result.** The first completion of (a) is searched only along the unbroken run of signals, in the order of (p), whose results are final. A signal with an open result stops the search: no later signal can end the block or be counted in it until that result is final, so a late result can never move a block's end backwards.
- (s) **What one signal is.** Each rule's registration or frozen sheet says what one signal is. Where it does not, it is one counted signal per rule, market and side until the registered exit or the settlement; a rule that fires again on a later poll of the same market and side has not made a second signal.
- (t) **After QUALIFIED** no further block is read for that place; its record from then on is its seat's. **After an expired block or confirmation** the next block starts at the first counted signal whose decision time is after the expiry time; the signal that arrives past the 21 x 24 hours is that first signal if it is counted.
- (u) **The offset window, row by row (ruled Nov 13, 2026 02:39Z on a difference Forensics found with a planted fixture).** The frozen sheet says a poll's exchange-time offset is the median over the 120 most recent rows of the same run folder 'by receive time, at or before the poll'. Where several rows share one receive instant (NC15 reads all its tickers in one batched poll), 'at or before the poll' means the rows up to and including that poll's OWN ROW, in the run's order: rows sorted by receive time with a stable sort, so rows of one instant keep the order the recorder wrote them. Two rows of one instant can therefore carry offsets that differ by hundredths of a second. This is the reading of the frozen, audited NC15 scorer (8d1055df), kept so that no hash moves; the other reading (one offset for every row of an instant) is not used. The effect is hundredths of a second and has no tie to any result.
- **Start of the count.** Because (f) was a choice still open in a frozen sheet after its places were admitted, the places that count on the NC15 log restart their forward count at the time the two independent builds first print the same records on the invented fixtures and this text is closed; that time is written into WHEEL_ADMIT_LIST.md then, as a UTC timestamp, and (l) says what is compared with it. v1.3 (Nov 13, 2026) removes the single-day test applied to a read block; it changes neither what a signal is nor which signals count, so it does not move a start time. Item (u), by contrast, closes a choice about the signal clock, so the NC15 places' start time moves once more, to the time written in WHEEL_ADMIT_LIST.md after (u) was committed. Signals before it do not count toward any block.

## 5. A seat

1. **10 contracts, fixed, at most $10 of cost an order (the owner, section 0).** No ladder, no profit add-on, no reinvestment. The registered exit cannot be changed while live. The second slot's existing caps still apply to each rule and scale with the sizing base; the smaller cap always wins. In code today (the Chief of Staff, Nov 12) those caps are fractions of the sizing base with fixed ceilings: 1 percent an order (ceiling $20), 2 percent a window ($40), 3 percent open in total ($60), 2 percent a day ($20), 6 percent in a rule's lifetime ($60). So an order that costs more than 1 percent of the base is refused, which means a full $10 order needs a base of $1,000 or more; the daily ceiling is two full $10 losses; and the lifetime ceiling equals the $60 kill, so at a smaller base the lifetime cap stops a rule before its kill line can be reached. The Chief of Staff checks all of this against the real figures before any admission (no account figure is written here).
2. **Live against shadow, first 20 live orders:** fewer than 60 percent filled, or an average fill more than 1c worse than the shadow's price for the same windows, and the rule leaves its seat with its shadow record marked overstated.
3. Label everywhere it is shown: "rotating (unproven)".

## 5a. Probation seats (the owner, section 0.10) [design]

A probation seat is a live seat taken WITHOUT a forward record. It exists because the owner wants the live minimum of 5 reached before the wheel's own records can exist. It is his decision and an exception to "no orders before evidence", for these seats only.

1. **At most 5 probation seats.** 3 contracts, at most $3 of cost an order.
2. **Who may take one:** a rule with a written registration (entry, side, band, look, limit, exit fixed before any outcome), that is not on the declined list, is not closed, and whose registered research read, where one has been made, is not under 1.0. A rule whose read comes back under 1.0 while it holds a probation seat leaves the seat that day. Favourites at 88c or above do not take probation seats: his rule for them is a record of 150 signals with 10 losses.
3. **Each one needs the owner's own popup with its exact spec** (market, side, band, look, limit, size, exit, kill), then the Chief of Staff's build, merge and security review, the check of the exchange shard that market trades on (an order needs money on its own shard), and the slot caps. Nothing here places an order by itself.
4. **Kill: an $18 drawdown from its own peak**, six times the most one order can lose, latched; return only by his popup. The two performance exits of section 6 apply as written (net under minus 1c a contract at 50 fills; the last 60 under 1.0), after which it goes to the shadow list like any other rule.
4a. **As built for Monday Nov 16 (the Chief of Staff, Nov 12):** a probation seat is an ordinary second-slot strategy. Its kill is a NET FLOOR of minus $18 (what the slot offers today), not a drawdown from its peak: the money a seat can lose from its start is capped at $18 either way, but a seat that first earns and then gives it back is not stopped until its net is minus $18. The two performance exits are not slot features and do not apply to probation seats until the replay controller exists; until then the net floor, the slot caps and the shared account stop are the protection.
5. **Stepping up to 10 contracts** happens only on the record every full seat needs: 60 fills at 1.3 with both halves at 1.0 and 10 of the rarer outcome, in non-overlapping blocks, then 30 more at 1.0, counted on its own live fills, which are fill-checked by nature. Until then it stays at 3.
6. **Labels.** Everywhere it is shown: "probation (no record)". Its fills are never research evidence, never quoted as a pass, and never used to choose a direction, a cut or a parameter of a research test that has not been read.
7. **What to expect, plainly.** A probation rule is chosen on reasoning and at best a development read on candles, not on a forward record; the published evidence read on Nov 12 supports none of the registered directions strongly. With no edge it loses about the fee, roughly 2c a contract an order, until its own lines turn it out: about $3 on average at this size, at most $18. Five seats can lose at most $90 before all five are killed. What they buy is live fill data and the start of a real record.

## 6. Leaving a seat [design]

Automatic, replayed from the rule's own settled orders on the cash basis. An open position is finished by the rule's own exit; the exit watch is not touched.

1. **A cash drawdown of $60 from the rule's own peak (a KILL: latched).** This is six times the most one order can lose ($10), as the owner asked: it cannot trip on one trade, or on five losses in a row at the most expensive price. Why six and not four or eight, on the coin flips: at four ($40) a real 1.3 rule is killed about two times in three within 200 fills; at six about one time in two; at eight barely less than at six, while a truly losing rule costs about a tenth more. A dead rule usually leaves on lines 2 and 3 before it reaches this one.
2. Net under minus 1c a contract at the 50th fill (a performance exit).
3. At every 60th fill, the last 60 fills under 1.0 (a performance exit).
4. The live-against-shadow check (a KILL: latched).

Alerts only, never an exit by themselves (the Refinement desk's review: on gold these fired mostly on a strategy that then kept earning, and saved nothing): a rolling 60 fill profit factor under 0.7; for a rule that exits early, 3 early sells in the last 40.

What lines 1 to 3 do on coin flips at 10 contracts: a rule with no edge leaves after about 50 fills at an average cost of about $10 to $13 (one time in twenty, about $50 to $60); a rule that truly loses (0.8) costs about $24 to $31. They also turn out a rule that truly has 1.3 within 200 fills about one time in two; such a rule earns about $55 to $95 over those fills. Real rules are rotated out by noise too and come back through section 7. A known cost of this design, from the Refinement desk: taking a rule off after its own bad stretch and putting it back after its recovery is timing its mean reversion, and on history that hurt rules that bounce.

## 7. Cooling and return

1. After a performance exit: 14 days out, then a new block made only of signals after the exit date, plus the confirmation, both on fill-checked rows. Then the rule takes a seat again automatically, **at most 2 times** (the owner, section 0).
2. **Never automatic after a kill.** A rule that left on its $60 drawdown or on the live-against-shadow check returns only by a reviewed re-arm: at least 30 days, a fresh block and confirmation, the owner's popup and the Chief of Staff's merge. The automatic return of line 1 is for a performance exit only (lines 2 and 3 of section 6). This is the Chief of Staff's security condition: an automatic return is acceptable only where the rule's registry entry already exists from admission, the state is replayed from rows, and size, shared stop and kill latches are unchanged.
3. **A third exit** of any kind: the rule stays out until the owner's popup (section 0). Research's recommendation is that a rule with three exits inside 90 days is retired to the declined list unless he says otherwise.

## 8. The total at risk

1. **There is no lane-wide pause (the owner, section 0).** Each strategy stands on its own $60 kill.
2. Wheel orders run in the second slot under its existing per-strategy caps, count in the shared 12 percent account stop and the 5.5 percent window cap, and obey the direction lock.
3. **The plain consequence (told to the owner before his approval; the real stop figure is set against it by the Chief of Staff before any admission):** with no seat maximum and no lane cap, the thing that limits the wheel's total loss on a bad day is the shared 12 percent account stop, and when that stop trips it ends the whole bot's session, the non-wheel strategies included, until it is started again. On the coin flips (30 shadows, 10 contracts, section 10) the wheel's worst single day in six months is typically about minus $35 to minus $45 and its worst week about minus $70. ## 9. Not just picking lucky shadows

1. Sections 3 and 4: forward signals only, a dated admit list, one set of parameters, blocks that do not overlap, the confirmation, fill-checked rows. **Seats per market multiply the looks:** thirty places on a dozen mechanisms are not thirty independent ideas, and two markets of one rule can look hot together because they share a cause. The ledger therefore counts every market place as its own row, and the notice for a seat names the sibling markets of the same rule and how they stand.
2. **A lane ledger** on the `rnd-queue` branch, kept by the Head of R&D: every rule ever admitted, every block read, cleared or failed. Each time a rule takes a seat the owner's notice says how many blocks the wheel has read so far and how many lucky seats that many blocks would produce with no edge anywhere (blocks times about 0.04, or 0.05 for a favourite at 88c or above). With 30 shadows that is about one to one and a half lucky seats a month.
3. **The lane scorecard is the real test of the wheel's premise**, which is that a good recent record says something about the next few weeks. After 150 wheel fills in total the wheel's combined cash profit factor is read once; under 1.0 the owner is told at once and decides. Nothing pauses by itself, since he chose no lane-wide pause. It is reported every Monday, profit factor first, with n.
4. A wheel record is never quoted as a research-bar pass or as proven. Any size above 10 contracts needs the proven bar on live fills and the owner's popup.
5. **The premise test comes before the first seat (the owner, section 0.7).** WHEEL0, one registered test with its templates, blocks and verdict lines fixed before any outcome is joined: when a block is hot, what do the next 60 signals show, against blocks that were not hot? It is run by the Forensics desk on the recorded order books, with public one-minute candles over the longer window for power, since two weeks of books hold only a handful of hot blocks; the label is "premise supported", "premise not supported" or "no evidence at n = x hot pairs", and no seat is taken before it is read. Everything in this file depends on the answer.

## 10. What to expect, plainly [design]

**One order.** At 10 contracts a rule that truly has 1.3 earns about 37c to 65c an order (most at mid prices, least near the ends). A losing order costs about $2 at 20c, about $5 at 50c and about $8 at 80c.

**One lucky fake.** A rule with no edge that reaches a seat costs about $10 to $13 before its own lines turn it out (one time in twenty, $50 to $60). That cost is the fees it pays; a rule that breaks even after fees costs about nothing on average.

**The wheel.** 30 shadows, no seat maximum, 10 contracts, each rule seeing about 4 signals a day with 70 percent fillable, on coin flips with the real fee:

| If the share of the 30 rules that truly have 1.3 is | wheel result a month | bad month (1 in 20) | seats filled on average | share of live orders from real rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | about minus $12 | about minus $36 | about 1 | none |
| 1 in 10 | about plus $44 | about minus $20 | about 2 | about half |
| 1 in 5 | about plus $113 | about minus $5 | about 3 to 4 | about two thirds |
| 2 in 5 | about plus $247 | about plus $115 | about 6 | most |

Two things follow. First, the wheel earns in proportion to how many of its rules are real, and research found none at the research bar on Nov 12, so the honest starting expectation is the top row or the second. Second, **five seats filled at once needs about two rules in five to be real** at this bar; with fewer, the wheel will often show one to three seats, and the only ways to fill five regardless are a looser bar (which seats luck) or many more shadows.

**What can feed it.** Research found no rule at the research bar on Nov 12. Rules with a written, registered spec today: 8 mechanisms (OICORE and EVENT830, already live under their own rules; a book-state rule on the Bitcoin and Ethereum 15 minute markets; a Kalshi-only Bitcoin model-edge rule; an esports break rule; an in-play price-jump rule; an e-soccer rematch rule; an e-soccer tie rule). In two weeks about 10 to 12. Twenty different mechanisms is about January at two or three new ones a week.

- **With seats per market (decided):** about 14 places this week from the six mechanisms that are not already live (the price-jump rule on five sports, the esports break on four games, the depth rule on two markets, and the rest), and 20 to 30 within about two weeks as the hourly temperature, period-tie and non-crypto depth rules are written. That is 20 to 30 places on about a dozen mechanisms, not 20 to 30 different ideas.
- A rule with negative evidence is still not admitted on any market, and a development read that falsifies a rule removes all of its market places at once.

Shadowing a rule costs no money, only logger reads (free for the four markets the bot already reads; one public read per look elsewhere, which needs sizing for series with hundreds of events a day) and the builder's time to register it.
