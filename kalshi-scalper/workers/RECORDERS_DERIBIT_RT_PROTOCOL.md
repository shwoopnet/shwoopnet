## DERIBIT1 (idea 8): Deribit-implied digital probability vs the Kalshi hourly Bitcoin ladder (KXBTCD)
Question: at the last minutes before an hourly close, are the cheap "above K" strikes (YES ask 3c to 30c) priced below the probability the options market implies, by more than the fee? Model fixed now: P(above K) = Phi((ln(S/K) - 0.5 s^2 t) / (s sqrt t)), S = Deribit BTC index, s = mark IV of the ATM option of the nearest expiry (annualised), t = time to the Kalshi close in years; trade rule for the test: buy YES at ask in [0.03, 0.30] when P > ask + fee + 0.02. Honest limit: Deribit's nearest expiry is the next 08:00 UTC, so s for a 1 hour horizon is a proxy; the earlier ladder tests (HISTLAD1, LADCONV1) priced from realized vol and found nothing: prior 5%.
Data (one CSV row per poll per strike, 7 strikes nearest the index): UTC time, index, option expiry and strike, mark IV of the call and put, Kalshi event and close time, strike, YES bid and ask. Public keyless GETs; no order.
Runner-minute estimate (each job bills at least 1 minute of setup plus its polling): 
| plan | windows | billed minutes | readable by | what it can answer |
| pilot: 1 close a day (20:00 UTC), 450 s window | 28 in 4 weeks | about 28 x 9 = 252 (at the 250 limit) | 2026-12-14 | feasibility only: 28 clusters cannot reach a PF 1.3 verdict (needs about 300 hourly windows) |
| full: all 24 closes a day, 450 s each | 336 in 2 weeks | about 336 x 9 = 3,024 | 2026-11-30 | a real verdict at z about 3 for a modest edge |
| mid: 6 closes a day | 168 in 4 weeks | about 1,512 | 2026-12-14 | marginal |
The repo is public so the minutes are free in money terms, but the Charter limit is about 250 minutes a month per recorder without the owner: only the pilot fits. The full plan needs an owner yes on about 3,000 minutes (the existing record-quotes and record-book recorders already use far more). I recommend the full plan or nothing: a 28-window pilot proves only that the poller works.
Draft: workers/drafts/record_deribit.py (copy to scripts/) and workers/drafts/record-deribit.yml (copy to .github/workflows/): keyless, pushes to the `deribit-data` branch only, concurrency group, a stop date of 2026-12-14 in the `if`, timeout 12 minutes, 3 failures in a row stops the poller (no retry beyond that, no worked-around rate limit). Not run, not tested against the live APIs end to end (the Deribit and Kalshi GETs were probed separately: Deribit index price answers 200).
## RT1 (idea 9): Rotten Tomatoes score ladders
Question: before release, do the cheap "score above X%" strikes lag the review-count curve? Kalshi has 240 RT series (one per film, ticker KXRT...). Data per daily snapshot: for each open RT market, its YES bid and ask, plus the film's public Tomatometer and review count from the film's RT page.
Runner estimate: 1 run a day, about 1.5 billed minutes, 4 weeks = about 42 minutes: well inside the 250 limit. Readable: slow, a verdict needs about 40 films with a settled score: 4 to 8 weeks. Prior 5%.
Open problems before a workflow can be written: (1) the film-to-page mapping: whether a market's rules text carries the RT URL is unverified (the one RT series I probed had no open market); step 0 is a 5-request probe of the open RT markets. (2) Terms: rottentomatoes.com/robots.txt allows generic agents on /m/ pages (Disallow only pictures, search, account paths) but its terms of service on automated access were not read; an owner or counsel check is advisable before a recorder scrapes it. No workflow draft until both are answered.
