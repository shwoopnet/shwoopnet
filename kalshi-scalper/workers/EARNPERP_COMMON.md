## Mechanism and counterparty
Single stock perps trade around the clock (Friday 17:00 ET to Sunday 18:00 ET excepted) while earnings are filed after the close (usually 16:00 to 16:30 ET) or before the open (usually 06:00 to 08:00 ET). Between the filing and the 09:30 ET cash open the perp is the only tradable price of the stock. Counterparty: holders of the cash stock who cannot trade it until it reopens and who accept a wide, thin overnight book to act on the release (the cost map shows the overnight book about 3 times wider than the midday one and a touch of about $1,000 to $3,000 on most names). The honest alternative is that the perp price a few minutes after the filing already IS the cash opening gap, so nothing is left to trade after the cost.

## Event definition (common)
Event = (single-stock perp, first 8-K Item 2.02 of a fiscal quarter) with EDGAR acceptance time R, session POST (acceptance 16:00:00 to 19:59 ET, release day a weekday) or PRE (04:00 to 09:24 ET). Intraday filings (09:25 to 15:59) are not events. Foreign issuers, ETFs and indexes are out. A POST filing on a Friday needs its look before the Friday 17:00 ET venue close, else skipped (counted). Quotes come from the 1 minute candles read at the read date at fixed New York clock minutes (a candle that ENDS at the stated minute; mid = (close bid + close ask) / 2; a minute with no bid or no ask close, or ask over twice the bid, is missing; a missing candle skips the event and is counted). Anchor A = mid at 16:00 ET of the last trading day on or before the release date (the cash close, whose 21:00Z funding stamp has just been set). Look L = R rounded up to the next minute plus 15 minutes, must be at most 09:15 ET (PRE) or before the 17:00 ET Friday close. Exit E = the candle ending 09:29 ET of the next trading day for POST, of the release day for PRE (the last minute before the cash open auction). m = ln(mid(L) / A) in basis points. Cost screen (ex ante, observable, chosen on cost not on returns): the close spread at L at most 20 bps (so the one or two contract touch is not a defect), else skipped and counted.
Fee: 0.12% of notional per side (placeholder, NOT confirmed against Kalshi's fee page), stress x1.2, break-even fee per side always reported. Net per trade = gross minus the crossing of the recorded bid and ask at L and E minus 0.24% round trip.

## Common verdict rules
Per-trade net as above. PF = sum of wins over sum of losses. Control = the same entries with the direction reversed.
- **NOT_ENOUGH_DATA:** fewer than 30 trades (events passing the screens with all three candles) or fewer than 20 distinct release dates at the read date; the date the bars would be met at the observed rate is reported.
- **FALSIFIED:** at least 30 trades on at least 20 dates and any of: PF under 1.5 in either half, net per trade not above 0 in both halves, PF under 1.2 at fee x1.2, trigger not better than control, date-clustered z under the deflated bar (about 5.32 at N about 1,230,525; with 30 to 50 trades only a very large effect can reach it, that is stated and accepted).
- **LEAD:** all pass; then the held-out part is read once, PF under 1.5 there is FALSIFIED.

## What forward recording would make these readable sooner (for the owner; nothing built)
The binding limit is the earnings calendar, not the data: names report in seasons (about 45 of 54 between mid-January and late February, then again late April to mid-May), and no recorder creates events. Everything the three tests read is already public and permanent (candles, funding history, EDGAR acceptance times, Yahoo daily bars), so none of them needs a recorder to be scored on the registered dates. What a recorder buys:
1. **Top-of-book sizes and the venue reference around each release** (the candles carry neither): one scheduled job at 60 seconds, weekdays 16:00 to 20:00 ET and 04:00 to 09:25 ET, one GET of `margin/markets` (all rows, same columns as `record_perps.py`), about 560 samples a day, about 4 to 5 runner hours a day, appended to `perp-data` only, about 60 KB gzipped a day, free runner minutes on a public repository. It gives depth at the touch in the 15 to 90 minutes after a filing, which is the only way EARNPERP2 can say how many contracts its spread applies to (today the answer is "the top of book, size unknown"). Only the weeks of events matter, but the job cannot know the day, so it runs every weekday; or the weekly Nasdaq calendar can pick the days if the owner wants it cheaper (about 10 to 15 event days a month in season).
2. **Cash pre- and post-market 1 minute bars on event days** (Yahoo keeps only about 7 days of 1 minute history): one nightly job, 58 GETs of the 5 day 1 minute chart with pre/post, appended to `perp-data`. Needed only for a later test that compares the perp to the cash AFTER-HOURS print, not for the three registered tests (which use the cash open and close from daily bars). About 3 minutes of runner a night.
3. **A weekly snapshot of the Nasdaq calendar** (about 30 GETs a week) so the scheduled session known at the time is kept (the calendar changes before the print). Negligible.
Cost of all three: GitHub Actions minutes only, no key, no order, nothing on main. It would NOT make the tests readable before the January season; it would make EARNPERP2 report depth, and keep the cash after-hours print for a second-generation test.
