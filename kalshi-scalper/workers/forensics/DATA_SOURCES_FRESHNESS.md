# Which recorded files are a delayed copy, and which read the order book (2026-11-12)

Asked for by the Head of R&D after QUOTEVSBOOK1. Written from the recorder scripts on `origin/main` (`scripts/record_*.py`), so it says what each recorder READS; only the first line of each group below was measured. Public data only, no live account figure.

**Measured.** Kalshi's market LIST read (`/markets?tickers=...`) returned the same quote for about 11 seconds at a time on the Bitcoin 15 minute market (72,012 polls, 4,360 distinct states; `QUOTEVSBOOK1_RESULT.md`). The ORDER BOOK read (`/markets/orderbooks?tickers=...`) was never the same from one 2 second poll to the next. So a price taken from a market object in a list is 0 to about 11 seconds old, about 5.5 on average; a price taken from the order book is current to the poll.

| Price is from | Recorder and folder | What it means for use |
|---|---|---|
| **The order book** (current) | R6, `book15r6/` on `quote-data` (five 15 minute series, 2 s) | Safe to price from. The freshness above was measured on this one. |
| | NC15, `nc15/` on `nc-data` (eleven non-crypto 15 minute series, 5 s) | Same endpoint as R6; not separately measured. |
| | Hourly Bitcoin ladder book, `book/` on `quote-data` (7 strikes, 3 s) | Per-strike order book: treat as current. Its choice of WHICH strikes to follow comes from a market list (a delayed mid), which matters only at the moment a strike is picked. |
| | Combo recorder, the `book` part on `combo-data` (best 5 levels of the 40 tightest) | Order book: current. |
| **Market objects in a list** (a copy up to about 11 s old) | Quote recorder, `quotes/` on `quote-data`: `quotes.csv` (15 minute crypto and gold, last 150 s) and `ladder.csv` (hourly and gold ladders) | NOT a price at the stamped second. This is the one that was measured. Do not price a rule from it or form a seconds-level signal on it. |
| | In-game recorder, `ingame/` on `sports-data` (moneyline, spread, total every 20 s) | Market objects from an event list. Assumed delayed the same way (not measured: 20 s polls cannot show an 11 s refresh). Any lag test finer than about 10 to 20 seconds on it is in doubt; whether markets of one event share one copy age is unknown. |
| | Daily (`daily/`), reference (`ref/`), weather (`wxens/`), rain (`raincal/`) on `quote-data`; politics on `pol-data`; the combo `quotes` part on `combo-data` | Market objects from a series list. A delay of seconds is harmless for markets read every 30 minutes or more. |
| **Not known** | The single-market read (`/markets/{ticker}`), used by the signal logger (`signals/`) and by the bot for its decision price | Not measured here. Refinement is checking it from order rows that hold both the quote seen and a book snapshot. |
| | Perps (`/margin/markets`), `perps30/`, `perps10/`, hourly on `perp-data` | A different list endpoint. **Still not measured**: PERPSLIST1 (2026-11-12) read "no evidence at n = 4" because only one 30 second file exists and its samples sit at fixed offsets (re-read about Nov 19, or after the first Sunday 10 second file). Fine for spread LEVELS (it agrees with the perp candles to about 1 basis point when read 5 s apart); not to be trusted to the second. The perp candles themselves are "assumed, not measured": no perp order book or tape is recorded. |
| **Cleared (measured current)** | Kalshi's 1 minute candles, bid and ask close, **for `KXBTC15M` only** | CANDLEVSBOOK1 (2026-11-12, accepted by the Head of R&D): the candle's ask close is the R6 book's ask at the minute's end. 95.9% of rows within 1c at close minus 120 s (bar 90%), 98.4% over all minutes, best time shift 0 s; a copy even 2 s old would have shown. Weaker in the last minute before the close (80%). One instant per minute, and the fill haircut still applies. **Any other series is "assumed, not measured"**: say so on the result, and make no seconds-level claim there without its own check. |
| **Not a quote** | The trade tape (`trades.csv` in R6, NC15, the hourly book and combo) | Each trade carries the exchange's own time, so it is exact whenever it is fetched. Safe as a record of prices paid. |
| | Deribit (`deribit/`) and OKX liquidations (`oklq/`) | Other venues; not covered by this note. |

**Rule of thumb.** Price from `book15r6/`, `nc15/`, `book/` or a tape. Treat any `yes_bid` or `yes_ask` that came out of a `/markets?...` list as "what the market looked like some seconds ago".

## Added 2026-11-12 after the Verification Analyst's check and TAPECLOCK1

- **Which side is old is now settled with a third clock.** Against the trade tape (the exchange's own time on each trade), the R6 book matches the price takers paid best when read within about a second of the trade (82% within 1c at 150 to 120 s before the close); the market-list quote matches it best only 7 to 8 seconds after the trade, and is within 1c of it at the trade's own time on a third of trades (`TAPECLOCK1_RESULT.md`).
- **The quote is worse nearer the close than the headline says.** Quote and book agree within 1c on 57% of rows as stamped at 150 to 120 seconds, and on 42% at 120 to 30 seconds; against the tape the quote is within 1c on 20% of trades in the last two minutes.
- **Book SIZES are not depth truth.** On R6 the size at every level, level 5 included, changes from one 2 second poll to the next (the Verification Analyst's finding), the touch itself has moved within half a second for 71% of trades, and 6% of prints at the touch are larger than the size shown half a second earlier. Use the book for PRICES. Treat a displayed size as "what was shown at that instant", not as what an order arriving later would meet, and never as an upper limit. The tape cannot confirm or refute sizes away from the touch.

## Rule added 2026-11-12 (LADDERSTATE1, accepted by the Head of R&D)

**Two list quotes read in the same second are not simultaneous.** Markets returned in one market-list response refresh at different times (on the hourly ladder file, in 70% of poll cycles some strikes had changed and some had not; in none had all changed). Each market's copy has its own age between 0 and about 11 seconds. So a lock, parity or cross-market comparison built on list quotes can show gaps that never existed at one instant, and must be confirmed on order book reads before it is believed. `ladder.csv` is now MEASURED as a list copy (changes every 10.2 to 10.3 s at the median), no longer "by the same endpoint, assumed".
