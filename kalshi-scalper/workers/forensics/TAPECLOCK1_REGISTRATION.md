# TAPECLOCK1 registration: the trade tape as a third clock, to say which of the quote and the book is the old one (2026-11-12, committed BEFORE anything was computed)

Asked for by the Head of R&D after the Verification Analyst's PARTLY CONFIRM of QUOTEVSBOOK1: the shift between the quote and the book does not by itself prove which side is old. Each trade on the R6 tape carries the exchange's own time and price, so it is a clock that belongs to neither recorder. A measurement: no rule scored, no settlement result loaded, zero tries. Public recorded data, zero Kalshi requests, nothing about the live account. Script `tapeclock1.py`.

**Constants.** `KXBTC15M`, closes from 2026-10-29T22:15:00Z up to but not including 2026-11-05T00:00:00Z (the QUOTEVSBOOK1 days). Tape trades from 150 to 30 seconds before the close (the quote recorder exists only in the last 150 s), shown for 150 to 120 s and for 120 to 30 s. A trade is used when the taker's own price for the side it bought (`yes_price_dollars` for a YES buy, 1 minus it for a NO buy) is from 0.05 to 0.30: the taker bought the cheap side, defined by the trade itself and by neither source. Only trades in windows that have both an R6 book and quote-recorder rows are used, so both sources are judged on the same trades.

**The comparison.** For a source S (the R6 book; the quote recorder) and a shift k in whole seconds from -12 to +12: the ask of the bought side in the last row of S received at or before (trade time + k), if that row is no more than 4 s older than that. For the book the ask is 1 minus the other side's best bid; for the quote recorder it is `yes_ask` for YES and 1 minus `yes_bid` for NO. gap = trade price minus that ask. Reported for every k: share of trades with |gap| at or under 1c, mean absolute gap, mean signed gap, per trade; at k = 0 with 95% intervals from resampling whole windows. Positive k means the source is read AFTER the trade.

**Reading rule, fixed now.** A source is "current" if its agreement with the tape peaks within 2 s of k = 0 (a taker pays about the ask that was there when it traded; the last 2 second poll before a trade is about 1 s old, so the book's peak may sit at 0 to +2). A source is "old by about L seconds" if its agreement peaks at k = +L with L of 3 or more: it only shows the price the tape traded at L seconds after the trade happened. One sentence states which side is old.

**Sizes (one line, said in advance to be weak).** For trades within 0.5 s after an R6 poll and at exactly that poll's ask for the bought side: the share whose size is at or under the size displayed at that ask, and the median of trade size over displayed size. This can show whether a displayed size is routinely EXCEEDED by a single print (so it is not an upper limit on what can trade); it cannot show whether a displayed size is real resting interest, because a size that is never hit leaves no trace on the tape.
