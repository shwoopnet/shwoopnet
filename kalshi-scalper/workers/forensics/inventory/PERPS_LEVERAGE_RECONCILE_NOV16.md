# Perps leverage and forced-close reconcile (Forensics desk, Nov 16, 2026; the Head's order). Public data and Kalshi's own pages only; no key.
Public requests used for this note: 2 (gold 1 minute candles 17:38Z to 17:52Z, gold market object) of the 5 approved; the fee-tier endpoint was NOT called (it needs an account key, see section 4).

## 1. Max multiplier versus size
- **Kalshi's pages state no tiers.** help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15357594-how-margin-works (June 3, 2026): "Initial margin is the amount you post to open a position. It is determined by the leverage you select and the notional size of your position." Single-stock page (https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/18183683-single-stock-perpetual-futures-on-kalshi): "Max leverage | Varies by stock, shown on each market page" and "The leverage available depends on the stock and your position size." No leverage-by-size table, margin rate by notional, or per-asset multiple is printed on any page I could read (kalshi.com/perpetuals/learn/futures-margins answered 429 and was not read).
- **What drives the size dependence (Kalshi's API docs):** the clearing members' maintenance margin metadata (https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/get-maintenance-margin-metadata.md, GET /margin/estimate_maintenance_margin/metadata, admin token, not public) lists scenario matrices (hvar, apc, aug, funding), "base_tail_percentile", "funding_tail_percentile" and per-market liquidation configs: market_impact_volatility, market_impact_coefficient, market_impact_exponent, market_impact_forecasted_volume, spread_rate. So margin is a tail-loss scenario number plus a liquidation cost with a market-impact term that grows with size: that is the likely reason the maximum multiplier FALLS with size. No numbers are printed.
- **The public API estimate (GET /margin/markets, fields leverage_estimate, long_leverage_estimates, short_leverage_estimates, "(1 / margin_rate)"), by notional key 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 / 1,000,000:** gold long 15.31 at every size, short 15.85; US500 long 14.48, short 12.10 at every size; US Tech 100 long 11.61, short 10.39; US 2000 long 10.04 to 10.03, short 11.17 to 11.16; BTC long 6.84 to 6.65, short 6.36 to 6.20; ETH long 4.88 to 4.71, short 4.38 to 4.24; silver long 8.16 to 8.09; copper long 9.05 to 9.00; stocks (e.g. AAPL, SPY) 4.96 flat. The API estimate falls with size only slightly (crypto, a little for metals), is flat for gold and the indices, and does NOT reproduce the page's maximums: gold 15.3 against 40x (page, tiny size) and 17.4x ($800 cost ticket); US500 12.1 to 14.5 against 36.1x; US Tech 100 10.4 to 11.6 against 28.9x; US 2000 10.0 against 24.6x; BTC 6.4 to 6.8 against 15.4x; silver 8.2 against 17.7x. Both columns are in the census TSV (page max = owner-pasted figures relayed by the Head, labelled as such; API estimate = size-aware).
**Reading:** the page maximum is a different quantity from leverage_estimate (the page figure is about 2 to 2.6 times larger, not a constant multiple: gold 2.6, US500 2.5, US Tech 100 2.5, US 2000 2.4, BTC 2.3, ETH 2.1, silver 2.2, copper 2.0). The docs do not say what leverage_estimate means beyond "(1 / margin_rate)"; whether it is a maintenance-based or a size-tiered rate is NOT settled by the docs.

## 2. Forced-close rule from the owner's gold ticket
Ticket (relayed by the Head): max multiplier 17.4x at an $800 cost, "Closes at GOLD $3,812.10", around 17:45Z. Gold's price at that time from the public 1 minute candles of KXGOLDPERP (contract = 0.001 ounce, so the per ounce price is 1,000 times the contract price): the candle ending 17:45Z has bid 3.8612 and ask 3.8616 (price 3.8616), i.e. gold about 3,861.2 bid and 3,861.6 ask per ounce; the market object at 17:48Z: bid 3,859.2, ask 3,859.6, last 3,859.6, reference 3,859.5, settlement mark 3,860.0, liquidation mark 3,859.6. The close price 3,812.10 is BELOW the entry, so the ticket is a LONG (assumed).
**Formula (long):** equity at price P = margin + notional x (P - P0) / P0, where margin = notional / L; the position is closed when equity falls to the maintenance margin m x notional (m = maintenance margin as a fraction of notional): P_close = P0 x (1 - 1/L + m), so **m = P_close / P0 - 1 + 1/L**. For a short, P_close = P0 x (1 + 1/L - m), so m = 1 + 1/L - P_close / P0.
**Inputs and result:** P0 = 3,861.6 (the ask, a long buys at the ask), P_close = 3,812.10, L = 17.4: P_close / P0 = 0.987182, 1/L = 0.057471, so m = 0.987182 - 1 + 0.057471 = **0.0447 (4.47% of notional)**, against an initial margin of 1/L = 5.75%: the buffer between the entry and the close is only 1.28% (49.5 dollars an ounce). Sensitivity: L is shown to 0.1, so 17.35 to 17.45 gives m 4.44% to 4.47%; entering at the bid (3,861.2) instead of the ask moves m by +0.01 point. If the ticket's "cost" is not the margin (for example it includes the fee), the result moves by that share. This is an INFERENCE from one ticket and an assumed long, not a Kalshi statement.
**Which price triggers it:** Kalshi's pages say only "If the market moves against your position and your account equity drops below the maintenance margin, your position is at risk of liquidation" (https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15357646-understanding-liquidation, Sept 26, 2026) and "Kalshi Klear places opposing market orders to close the defaulting position as quickly as possible". They do NOT name mark, liquidation mark or last price as the trigger. The market object carries a field named liquidation_mark_price (and settlement_mark_price, reference_price), so the liquidation mark is the likely trigger, but the docs do not say so. What one more public read would settle it: none of the public endpoints gives a position's liquidation price; only the owner's own ticket or position page can be compared with liquidation_mark_price over time (record it, see section 3).

## 4. Fee in basis points
GET /margin/fee_tier_rates (https://docs.kalshi.com/margin-rest/fees/get-fee-tier-rates.md) "Returns the fee schedule that applies to the authenticated margin-enabled account": it needs the KALSHI-ACCESS-KEY, KALSHI-ACCESS-SIGNATURE and KALSHI-ACCESS-TIMESTAMP headers (a signed account request, a 403 for an account that is not margin-enabled). I hold no key and never sign, so it was NOT called. The page prints only examples: "(e.g. 0.0005 = 0.05% = 5 bps)" for the maker rate and "(e.g. 0.0012 = 0.12% = 12 bps)" for the taker rate: illustrative, not the schedule. A web search returned unverified snippets of CFTC filings (cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09222628713.pdf and others): entry tier taker 12 bps (steps down with 30 day volume: 10, 8, 6, 5, 4 bps), entry tier maker 2 bps for an FCM schedule, crypto taker 4 bps (0.0400%); which applies to stock perps is not confirmed. The owner's own fee page or order ticket settles it (the fee tier shown to the account).
