## Why these three: the gap each one fills (from the VA's closed lists of tonight)
The record closes the 15 minute tape, the hourly ladder surface, locks and parity, flow and book shape, cheap-tail and fair-value forms. Each source below supplies what is missing: a dated list of EVENTS.

## 1. SEC EDGAR: earnings release times for single stock perps
- **Source and host:** the SEC's EDGAR submissions API, `data.sec.gov` (per company `submissions/CIK##########.json`, which carries each filing's form type, `filingDate` and `acceptanceDateTime`), and `www.sec.gov` for the daily index. An 8-K Item 2.02 ("results of operations") is the earnings release for most companies.
- **Terms (unverified):** US government data, public; the SEC's "fair access" policy asks for no more than 10 requests per second and a User-Agent that identifies the requester WITH contact details. **That conflicts with this repo's standing rule that recorders send a generic User-Agent with no personal details.** Main must read the policy and the owner must decide what company-level identification (the LLC's name and a company mailbox, not a personal address) is acceptable before any request.
- **What it makes testable:** stock perps around earnings (the Head's entry). Most earnings are released after the 4 PM ET close, while Kalshi's stock perps trade 24/7 against a closed cash market, so a labelled event list lets a test ask whether the perp's after-hours move at the release predicts the next regular-session move or the next day's perp. This is an EVENT mechanism on a 24/7 contract, with the event list as the new input. (OVN1 to OVN3 asked an overnight question without release labels and had 3 to 4 events.)
- **Events a year:** about 4 per company; for the 40 to 60 single stocks I assume are listed (the count is Main's to confirm from the perps list), about 160 to 240 a year. About 40 to 60 a quarter, so a first read of about 50 events takes roughly one quarter.
- **Recorder cost (estimates):** the submissions file per name, polled in earnings weeks only (about 6 weeks a year) every 30 minutes from 20:00 to 02:00 UTC: 50 names x 12 polls x 30 days = about 18,000 requests a quarter, on a GitHub runner about 1 billed minute a poll: about 12 x 30 = 360 minutes a month in earnings months and about 0 otherwise. The perps side needs the existing 30 second recorder (`perps30`) extended past its two windows to the after-hours release time, about 2 more hours a day of the same public read.
- **Live use:** none without an owner popup; stock perps need an application and the owner's account is personal (SNAPBACK1 note). Research only.

## 2. Official US release calendars and actuals (BLS, BEA, Treasury, Federal Reserve): event labels for minute-resolution ladder and event tests
- **Source and hosts:** `api.bls.gov` (CPI, jobs, PPI, JOLTS and others, with a published schedule), `apps.bea.gov` (GDP, PCE; an API key is required), `home.treasury.gov` (already read once a day by `marketTapeTick`), `www.federalreserve.gov` (FOMC calendar). Release calendars are published a year ahead on each agency's site.
- **Terms (unverified):** US government statistics are generally public; the BLS API has a daily request cap that is lower without a registered key; the BEA API needs a key (a credential, which goes through `request_credential`, never chat). Main to read each.
- **What it makes testable:** the VA's open ground (a): directional or event rules at MINUTE resolution on hourly, daily and weekly ladders around scheduled releases, with the release second and the released value as labels. Today the research has the market side (candles, ladders, the 15 minute series) but no joined release label or actual value, so EVENT830's release windows are a hand-written list and NEWH10 measured only "width follows the clock". Closest existing: EVENT830 and NEWH10, so any rule is an EVENT830 version unless it uses a different market or information source; the VA must rule on that first.
- **Events a year:** about 90 release dates across the four agencies (CPI 12, jobs 12, PPI 12, JOLTS 12, PCE 12, GDP 4 to 12, FOMC 8, others), of which perhaps 40 have Kalshi markets near the release. Too few events for a PF claim before about a year; enough for a measurement of repricing at minute resolution.
- **Recorder cost (estimates):** actuals only need a request after the release: about 90 events x 3 polls = about 300 requests a year; the calendars are fetched once a month. Runner minutes: effectively 0 to 30 a month if it runs only on release days.

## 3. ClinicalTrials.gov: a census of trial dates against Kalshi's clinical and health markets
- **Source and host:** the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, `clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies` (primary completion dates, status changes, results postings).
- **Terms (unverified):** run by the US National Library of Medicine, public; the site publishes usage guidance (a request-rate guideline). The owner's popups 41 to 44 already named a clinical trials family with "census first, terms read before any automated read", so this memo only supplies the SOURCE for that census; the family itself belongs to its owner (Desk C or the Head).
- **What it makes testable:** whether a registry status change or a posted result precedes the move in a clinical or health market, and how the market prices a trial whose registry date has already passed (a decay or event mechanism on a long-dated market, not the 15 minute tape). No registered test touches it.
- **Events a year:** unknown until the census says how many Kalshi markets exist and how many map to a registry record; I will not guess a figure. The 90 day hold limit in the owner's rules caps the usable ones.
- **Recorder cost (estimates):** the census is a one-time read (tens to low hundreds of requests); a daily poll of the records behind live Kalshi markets is under 100 requests a day (about 30 runner minutes a month at one billed minute a day).
