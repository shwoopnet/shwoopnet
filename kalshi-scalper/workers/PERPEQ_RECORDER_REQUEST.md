# PERPEQ recorder request (for the owner to decide; nothing here was built or changed; 2026-11-06)

## What exists today
- Hourly recorder (`record-perps.yml`, cron `7 * * * *`): GitHub's scheduler runs it 3 to 7 times a day at irregular times. About 4 samples a day have active equity books, one of them (about 14:56Z in the last day) inside US cash hours. It records top of book, top sizes, price, reference_price, marks, open interest, volume, funding cap and deadband.
- 30 second recorder (`record-perps-fast.yml`, 09:10 to 10:30 and 15:40 to 17:00 New York, stops 2026-12-07): the files are on origin/main (PR 725, 2026-11-05) but as of 2026-11-06 16:15Z **it has 0 workflow runs and no `perps30/` file exists anywhere**. Please check the Actions tab: a first-day delay is normal for a new scheduled workflow, but if it has not run by Monday 2026-11-09 09:10 ET, run it once by hand (`workflow_dispatch`).

## The important find: most of what the tests need is already public and stored
`GET /trade-api/v2/margin/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks?period_interval=1` (or 60) returns the CLOSE bid and ask of every minute since the market's first quote, with history that is not lost (US500 to at least 2026-10-26 at 1 minute; stock perps from 2026-11-05 12:46Z). No key. So a bid and ask at any clock minute can be read at the read date: **PERPEQ1, PERPEQ2 and PERPEQ4 need no new recording at all** (about 125, 20 and 6 trades expected in the development window; the cluster bar of 30 dates for PERPEQ1 is reached about 2026-12-21). The risk is only retention: if Kalshi trims the 1 minute history, it cannot be recovered. The cheapest insurance is below (item 1).

## What the candles do NOT carry (and the recorder must)
No sizes, no reference price (so no premium to the reference), no funding rate, no marks. PERPEQ3 (premium to reference in cash hours) is the only test that needs them, and the touch size ($1,000 to $3,000 on most names) is needed to say how many contracts any result applies to.

## Requests, in the order of value per effort (the owner picks)
1. **Nightly candle archive (cheapest, protects against retention).** One scheduled job a day (for example 02:00 UTC, after the Friday close each week too): for all 61 equity, ETF and index perps (and the 6 metals and WTI), GET the last 24 hours of 1 minute candles (about 1,440 rows each, 22 hour windows if the endpoint refuses a day) and append gzipped CSV to `perp-data/candles1m/<date>.csv.gz` with columns ticker, end_ts, bid_close, ask_close, open_interest, volume. About 68 GETs a day, about 1 to 2 MB a day gzipped (about 40 MB to 2026-12-31). Keyless, no order, no key. Read date: immediately useful; every PERPEQ test is then readable from the archive and the venue's own history.
2. **Cash-hours book snapshots with sizes: every 5 minutes, 09:30 to 16:00 New York, Monday to Friday, all 88 rows, same columns as `record_perps.py`.** One GET per sample (the list call returns every market), 78 samples a day, about 100 KB a day gzipped. This gives reference_price, sizes and premium at 5 minute resolution in cash hours, so PERPEQ3 goes from about 12 to about 150 usable trades per 15 days (it needs entry and exit samples at least 90 minutes apart; a 5 minute grid gives about 70 a day per perp). Run it as one long job (a loop with sleep inside a 6 hour job started by one cron at 13:15Z and 14:05Z, the way the quote recorder runs 4 hour jobs back to back), NOT as a `*/5` cron: GitHub drifts a `*/5` cron by 10 to 40 minutes and the hourly recorder is the proof.
3. **Pre-open and overnight book, every 60 seconds, 04:00 to 09:25 New York and 17:00 to 20:00 New York, Monday to Friday, plus Sunday 17:30 to 19:30 New York** (the venue's reopening; the live schedule shows the Friday 17:00 ET close). Same row format, only the 21 universe tickers (the universe changes only by a reviewed edit). About 340 samples a day, about 60 KB gzipped. Purpose: sizes and reference_price in the overnight book (about 3 times wider than midday) and the Monday reopening gap, which the candles show only as bid and ask.
4. **Keep the existing 30 second windows (09:10 to 10:30, 15:40 to 17:00) and extend `STOP_DATE` from 2026-12-07 to 2027-02-26** if CASHOPEN1, CASHCLOSE1 and CASHGAP1 are to reach their 150 trade bars (their registered dates: CASHCLOSE1 about 2026-12-08, CASHGAP1 about 2027-01-15, CASHOPEN1 about 2027-02-26); a reviewed edit of the stop date, nothing else. The candle archive in item 1 makes the 30 second windows unnecessary for any test that only needs bid and ask (1 minute is enough for every PERPEQ test); they matter only for sizes and sub minute lag.
5. **Depth beyond the top of book: no endpoint is documented keyless here** (the market object carries top sizes only). If `GET /margin/markets/{ticker}/orderbook` exists and is public, a 5 second snapshot of 5 levels of the 6 cheapest names in the first 10 minutes after 09:30 would show how deep $1,000 to $3,000 really is; I did not probe it and recommend a one-off probe before any build.

## What each item buys
- Item 1 alone: all of PERPEQ1, 2, 4 readable on any date, PERPEQ3 at 4 samples a day (NOT_ENOUGH_DATA until about mid 2027).
- Items 1 and 2: PERPEQ3 readable at the registered read date 2026-12-01 with about 150 trades; the premium behaviour per hour of day (the cost map by hour, one observation per hour bucket today).
- Items 1, 2 and 3: the overnight spread map by hour, and an overnight version of PERPEQ3 / OVN1 with real reference and size (needs a new registration; the frozen reference makes the overnight premium equal to the gap).
- Item 4: the three CASH tests; its only cost is Actions minutes.

## Cost
Keyless public GETs; GitHub Actions minutes only (item 1: about 5 minutes a night; item 2: about 6.5 hours a trading day of runner time, about 140 hours a month, well inside the free minutes for a public repository); all data goes to the `perp-data` branch only (never main, never published with the site); nothing here holds a key or places an order, and no trading code reads it. I did not build, edit or run any recorder.
