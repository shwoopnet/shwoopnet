# What published work says about the direction of four registered shapes (Idea Desk C, Nov 12, 2026)

For the Head of R&D. This changes no registration; directions stay as registered. No Kalshi request was made. [research]

**Counts:** 4 shapes; 26 sources found; 9 verified from the full text (the rest from abstracts or summaries, marked "abstract"); 4 practitioner sources, labelled. Results:
- Shapes with published support for the registered direction: 2 (in-play jump, partly; short-period tie, weakly).
- Shapes where the evidence points the other way or nowhere: 2 (pre-match move, rematch).
- Shapes with a published SIZE large enough for the bar (about 11 to 12 points at mid prices): 0.

## 1. Following a pre-match move in the last hour (PREMOVE buys the riser)

- **Gandar, Dare, Brown and Zuber (1998), J. Finance 53(1), NBA spreads (abstract):** moves go toward the outcome and remove the opening-line bias BY the close. The information is used up at the close, with none left past it.
- **Simon (2024), Management Science 70(12), 3,681 MLB games, four books (abstract):** line changes are NEGATIVELY autocorrelated, so they partly reverse. Weekend day-game closing lines forecast worse than lines from 90 minutes earlier.
- **Law and Peel (2002), Economica 69, UK racing (abstract):** a shortening price beats its close only when insiders drive it. When the shortening is herding, the bettor loses.
- **Geegeez (practitioner, 2018 to 2022, racing):** steamers win more often than drifters, but lose MORE against their closing price (the strongest steamers lost about 13p per pound).
- **Verdict:** against continuation past the close. The direction is either none or reversal. No size is given in probability points.
- **Odds for PREMOVE as registered:** under 1%.

## 2. Overreaction to the last head-to-head result (REMATCH buys the previous loser after his price fell)

- **No direct study of head-to-head recency exists.**
- **Swartz et al., NBA and NHL playoffs 2003 to 2011, over 1,200 games (verified):** the line marks the previous game's winner DOWN by about 1.25 probability points in the NBA, and less in the NHL. That describes pricing, not mispricing.
- **NBA playoff zig-zag (practitioner):**
  - 55.5% against the spread in 1991 to 2000 (Marc Lawrence).
  - 50% since 2005 (Action Network, 464 to 460).
  - It decayed to nothing.
- **Wheatcroft (2020), JQAS 16(3), 20 soccer leagues over 12 seasons (verified):** odds overreact to a team's recent run against ANY opponents. The extreme deciles were profitable, at a few percent.
- **Verdict:** the general recent-form overreaction is the same direction as REMATCH, but a few percent at the extremes. There is no evidence for the head-to-head version.
- **Odds for REMATCH:** about 1%.

## 3. Following a sharp in-play jump (INPLAYJUMP follows the jump)

- **Croxson and Reade (2014), Economic Journal 124, 1,206 Betfair soccer matches, second by second (verified):** the scorer's price jumps about 22 points, then shows no significant drift. The update is swift and full.
- **Choi and Hui (2014), JEBO 107, 2,017 Betfair matches (abstract):**
  - Most goals: UNDERreaction, a continuation, the registered direction.
  - Surprising underdog goals: OVERreaction, a reversal.
  - Corrected within minutes.
  - Strategy return 2.8% after commission at 2 minutes and 0.8% at 6 minutes (from a press summary, not the paper).
- **Easton and Uylangco (2010), IJF 26, Betfair tennis (abstract):** the market underreacts after a player concedes a break, so continuation. No size.
- **Norton, Gray and Faff (2015), JBF 61, Betfair one-day international cricket (abstract):** first-innings OVERreaction and second-innings underreaction. The authors call the returns "large", with no size seen.
- **Gil and Levitt (2007), Intrade, 50 World Cup matches (cited in Croxson and Reade):** 10 to 15 minutes of continuation. These markets were very thin.
- **Verdict:**
  - The registered direction (follow) has support in thin markets and for unsurprising events.
  - Liquid markets show no drift.
  - Surprising events reverse.
  - The largest return found is 2.8%, which is far under the bar. INPLAYJUMP's entry is the next minute, while the published edge has mostly gone by minute 2 to 6.
- **Odds for INPLAYJUMP:** about 1% to 2%. The thin, Kalshi-like venues are the case for it.

## 4. A tie in a short period against its base rate (TIE3 buys the Tie when under the base rate)

- **Franck, Verbeek and Nuesch (2010), IJF 26, 5,478 Big Five soccer matches (verified):**
  - Full-time draw observed 28.1%, against 28.0% implied by both Betfair and the bookmakers: fair on average.
  - A longshot pattern inside draws: high-priced draws are a little cheap, long draws are dear.
- **Angelini, De Angelis and Singleton (2021), 1,004 EPL matches on Betfair (verified):** wins are overpriced against the draw before kick-off (a "splitting" bias, so the draw is underpriced). No ROI is given.
- **Whelan (2026), 200,622 Betfair matches (verified):** pre-match home, draw and away prices are unbiased (p = 0.21).
- **Lezana (2026), J. Sports Economics 27(5), EPL (abstract):** claims abnormal returns "particularly for draws". No number seen.
- **Practitioner, NHL:** 22.4% of 7,945 games went past regulation tied (2020-21 to 2026). UK books' regulation-tie prices imply about 22% to 33%, so the tie is if anything OVERpriced there. No backtest.
- **Half-time, period, quarter and map ties:** nothing peer-reviewed.
- **Verdict:** the full-time draw is fair on average, with weak evidence of a small underpricing on exchanges. Nothing exists for short periods. The registered direction has weak support, and no size near 10 points was found.
- **Odds for TIE3:** about 1% to 3%. It is untested ground, so a measurement is the only evidence.

## One line for the owner

Published work supports the registered direction weakly for two of the four shapes (in-play jump, short-period tie) and not at all for the other two (pre-match move, rematch). None of the four has a published effect anywhere near the 11 to 12 probability points the bar needs at mid prices. Every registered direction rests on reasoning, not evidence, and the odds should be stated at 1% to 3% each.
