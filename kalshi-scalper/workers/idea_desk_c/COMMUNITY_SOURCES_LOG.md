## The Head's floor rule: three best dropped claims, and what each would need

1. **Speech-mention base rates (rows 14 and 50).**
   - **Source:** White House transcripts on whitehouse.gov, US government works, so no copyright bar. The terms page still has to be read before any automated read.
   - **Events:** mention markets settle about 26 event dates in 60 days, each with 10 to 25 word contracts. The day-clustered count is about 26 a cheap window and about 150 a year, so the result can falsify, never pass.
   - **Cost:** unmeasured for mentions; Forensics has no row.
   - **Size:** measured at scale (Bartlett and O'Hara, 4.5 points) is under the 11 to 12 the bar needs.
   - **Verdict:** a paper-list candidate at best, and inside MENT1 and MENTPERSIST1's family.
2. **Hourly city temperature settles on The Weather Company (row 21).**
   - **Events:** about 32,000 a year (87 on Nov 10). Settles 30 to 90 minutes after the close.
   - **Source:** the settlement source is The Weather Company. Its consumer site's terms are unread, and its data API is a paid licence. A permitted alternative is NWS station observations at the same airport, which are not the settlement source; the gap between the two is the open question.
   - **Cost:** unmeasured.
   - **Family question:** any rule that reads an observation is the WXDAYEND mechanism, and a forecast rule is the closed weather family. So this needs the Head's family ruling and the owner's word on a paid licence before it is a card.
3. **AI leaderboard markets read from the upstream leaderboard (row 12).**
   - **Source:** the public leaderboard site and its published dataset; terms unread.
   - **Events:** a slow family, weekly or monthly resolutions, under 52 a year.
   - **Cost:** unmeasured.
   - **Mechanism:** a race against the displayed page's refresh (claimed 15 minutes).
   - **Verdict:** paper list at best, and only if the terms allow automated reads.
