# ROUND1L (registered Nov 9, 2026, before any outcome is read): are round-number strikes on the hourly Bitcoin ladder mispriced?
Research only; local data `data/hist15/KXBTCD.sqlite` (14,270 chosen strikes, Jun to Aug 2026, minute candles). Mechanism and counterparty: retail anchors on round milestone levels (80,000, 79,500); a strike at a round number may attract extra buying of YES (or of NO) and be priced away from its fair chance, against makers with models. (A different test from the earlier ROUND1 note about fee rounding, which is L1 upkeep.)
Decision quote (fixed): the last candle at least 45 minutes before the market's close (2,700 s), both sides quoted (bid > 0, ask < 1, spread at most 5c); price paid YES at the ask, NO at 1 - bid; fee ceil(0.07 x 10 x p x (1-p) x 100)/10 cents per contract (C = 10); won = result. Cells: roundness class (strike nearest 1,000: "k1000" when round(strike) is a multiple of 1,000; "k500" when a multiple of 500 and not 1,000; "other") x side x price band (0.05 to 0.10, ..., 0.90 to 0.97) = 60 potential cells. Cuts: events (the hour) in time order, first two thirds development, last third held out and read ONCE for shortlisted cells. Shortlist: 100 development markets in 30 events, net edge >= 2c per contract after the fee, event-cluster z >= the one-sided normal quantile for 0.05/N with N = 4,440 (single-leg ledger so far) + 60 = 4,500 (z 4.26). Also reported as information: per roundness class the mean edge over all bands and the round-minus-other difference with its event-clustered z (one registered comparison, 1 try, bar z 2.0). Prediction: no shortlisted cell (about 93%); round strikes are not priced differently on this feed.
