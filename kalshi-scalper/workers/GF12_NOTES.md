# GF12 notes: how the GF1 and GF2 scorer reads the registration (V2 worker GF12, 2026-10-30, research only)

The scorer is `src/scalper/gf12.py` (`count`, `run gf1`, `run gf2`), tested on a synthetic fixture only (`tests/test_gf12.py`: made-up polls, tape and settlements). Built before any R6 outcome was read: no `result` column, no coverage file and no settlement was opened while building or testing. `count` reads R6 **book** files only (prices; the book has no result column), never coverage or trades. The registered rules (`workers/GOLDFILL_PROPOSAL.md`, R6T's shared rules) are not changed. Where the proposal leaves something open, the reading below is the one the scorer uses; this file is pushed before the scorer can run (it refuses before 2026-11-14 00:00 UTC). The lead can overrule any item before then.

## Readings

1. **GF2's z gate ("the per-window difference, GF2's entries against plain L1 on the same windows").** Two readings exist. (a) On each window GF2 entered, GF2's P/L per contract minus plain L1's on that window: both settle on the same result, so this only measures what waiting 30 s costs in price, and it is negative by construction (the prediction says GF2 pays ask(S) + 0.2c to + 0.8c), so it cannot test the filter. (b) The gate's own first clause: GF2's net per contract on its entries minus plain L1's net per contract on ALL signals of the same windows. **The scorer gates on (b)**, with the date-clustered z of that difference computed by linearising it per window (each signal window contributes `GF2 P/L / GF2 contracts` if entered, minus `plain P/L / plain contracts`; the sum is exactly the difference, and `mm1.clustered_z` on n times those terms gives the z clustered by US Central date). Reading (a) is reported beside it as information ("same-window difference"). This is the one place the scorer did not take the harder reading, because (a) would fail the gate by construction rather than by the data.
2. **Resting window.** Live at S + 1.0 s inclusive; cancelled at S + 31.0 s, exclusive (an event stamped exactly S + 31.0 s does not fill), as L1Q1's expiry is exclusive.
3. **A bid that is marketable when it goes live** (the side ask at or under L at a poll at or after S + 1.0 s) fills the whole remainder at L, our price, never at the better ask (R6T's shared rule "at OUR price").
4. **Queue.** "The first poll after it is live" is read as the first poll at or after S + 1.0 s (L1Q1's code). The size ahead is the larger of the displayed size at exactly L on our side's bids at S and at that poll. No level at L (L between recorded levels, or L above the best bid when the spread is over 1c) is 0 ahead. L under all 5 recorded levels of a full side (5 levels shown) on either poll is infinite (only a print or quote through L fills). Fills by `goldfill.rest_fill`: taker sales at L past the queue (partial fills kept), a sale strictly under L, a taker buying our side at or under L, or a recorded side ask at or under L, the last three filling the whole remainder at L.
5. **L and the cap.** L = ask(S) - 0.01 rounded to 0.001 (side price); the chase cap is ask(S) + 0.01. The chase (GF1) and the entry (GF2) are at the first poll at or after S + 31.0 s with no lateness bound (usable windows have gaps of at most 20 s); they walk only the recorded levels priced at most the cap, and size beyond those levels (including beyond the 5th level) is not bought and is counted.
6. **GF2's dip test** is GF1's resting bid at C = 1 by the same rules; "fully filled at C = 1" decides both sizes.
7. **Scored windows.** GF1 posts an order at every signal, so its count is the signals in usable windows. GF2's count is the windows with an entry at C = 1 (the capped cross is the same decision at C = 10). GF2's bar statistics (PF, thirds, halves, net per window, z, stress) are over its entry windows; GF2's gate thirds are thirds of all signal windows by close time (GF2 per contract on the entries in that third against plain L1 per contract on all signals in that third; a third with no entry fails).
8. **Usable windows** are L1Q1's coverage rule (`r6tests.usable("l1q1", ...)`), and only windows opening before 2026-11-13 23:59 UTC, the recorder's registered end, even if the recorder is left running.
9. **Card gates.** GF1: GF1's net per opportunity above plain L1's in every third at both sizes, and gold alone (all thirds pooled) above plain at both sizes. GF2: item 7's thirds and item 1's z at least 2.0, and gold alone (pooled) per contract above plain L1's per contract on gold's signals, at both sizes. Either gate failing makes the card FALSIFIED (with at least 300 scored windows), as the proposal says ("in addition to the bar").
10. **Fees.** The resting leg is one order on its filled quantity at L; the chase and GF2's entry are each one crossing order over their levels; each rounded up to the cent. Stress multiplies each rounded order fee by 1.2 (as `r6tests`). The information rows (maker 1.75%, zero) change the resting leg only.
11. **One run, and when.** `run` writes `data/gf12_<card>_started` after the date check and BEFORE any coverage row (which holds `result`) is read; a marker without a result file refuses again, so a crashed run is never quietly read twice (the lead decides). After the read date a run always writes a result, NOT_ENOUGH_DATA below 300 (the data can no longer grow then). `autoscore.py` fires it from 2026-11-14 02:00 UTC (two hours for the recorder's last 4 hour job to push the final windows) once `count` shows at least 300 signals, or from 2026-11-15 00:00 UTC whatever the count. This is scheduling only, not a rule.
12. **NOT_RUNNABLE** (a book schema change, or over 20% of usable windows without a clock) is written as the result, so it also counts as the one run.
13. **Signal.** L1Q1's rule exactly (exactly one side's ask in 0.88 to 0.97 at S). GOLDFILL's model broke a tie toward YES, which cannot arise (both asks cannot be at or above 0.88).
