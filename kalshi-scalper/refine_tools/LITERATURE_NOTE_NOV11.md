# Literature note: published edges on prediction markets (2026-11-11)
Method: 6 web searches, search summaries only. No page was opened, so every figure is second hand and must be checked against the primary paper. Nothing here is a finding for our bot.

## Claims
1. Maker-taker transfer on Kalshi. Becker, "prediction market microstructure" ([mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure), 2025). Claim: 72.1m trades, $18.26b, takers about -1.12% excess return, makers +1.12%; finance category gap 0.17%, media and world events over 7%; sign was reversed early on and flipped after 2024 as professional makers arrived. Data: Kalshi public trades with taker side. Testable now: yes, from the public trade tape (taker side is in each trade). Mechanism: takers pay for immediacy and longshot demand, makers collect. Distinct from favourite-late: yes (it is a who-trades-against-whom split, not a price band). Replication risk: medium (summary only, regime changes over time, a maker must also get filled and bear adverse selection).
2. Longshot bias on Kalshi. Burgi, Deng, Whelan, "Makers and Takers" ([CEPR DP20631](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:20631), [paper](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf), 2025). Claim: average return about -20% on Kalshi contracts (as reported by search summary, unverified); low-priced contracts lose most. Becker: a 5c contract wins 4.18%. Data: settled markets and prices. Testable now: yes. Mechanism: favourite-longshot bias (misperceived small probabilities, [Snowberg and Wolfers 2010](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15923), 5.6m US horse starts, misperception beats risk-love). Distinct from favourite-late: NO, this is the same family (buying the 88c to 97c side is the favourite end of it). Already ours. Replication risk: low that the bias exists, high that it survives fees.
3. Price-band and category calibration. [Kalshi Research calibration](https://kalshi.com/research/publications/calibration) (2026, self published): 2.24m resolved markets, 11 categories, accuracy 88.3% at 3 months to 97.2% at close; finance, politics, elections best. [Subramanian, Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/info/inarticle.php?artid=157002): 103,003 markets, 16 categories, volume-weighted payoff regressions show category and horizon specific distortions. Testable now: yes (public settled markets). Mechanism: bias differs by category and time to close. Distinct: partly (cross-category, cross-horizon map, not a single band). Replication risk: medium, authors differ in method.
4. Options-implied vs binary price. [Stevens project](https://fsc.stevens.edu/event-contract-mispricing-via-options-implied-probabilities/) (SPX, Breeden-Litzenberger and GBM), [BSIC crypto contracts](https://bsic.it/well-can-they-predict-an-investigation-of-crypto-prediction-contracts/), [arXiv 2604.01431](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01431v1) (Kalshi macro contracts vs crypto volatility), and an arXiv paper on Polymarket Yes prices running 5.6 points above Binance-implied values, largest at low probability and long expiry. Data: options implied vol (needs a vol feed, Deribit public is free) plus Kalshi prices. Testable now: partly. Mechanism: price a binary as a digital option and trade the gap. Distinct: yes. Replication risk: high (risk-neutral vs physical probability, fees, liquidity; the 5.6 point figure is Polymarket, which is out of scope).
5. Macro release accuracy. [Wright, Federal Reserve FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf) (Kalshi beats Bloomberg consensus on headline CPI, similar on core CPI and unemployment); [Kalshi Research, CPI](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6287792) (40% lower error over 25 months, self published); [Goel, Glasgow](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/390184/1/390184.pdf) (implied IQR before release). This says prices are good forecasts, which argues against an edge. No leakage study found. Distinct, testable now, but a negative result for trading.
6. Cross-venue lead-lag. [SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5331995): Polymarket leads Kalshi, large-trade net order imbalance predicts later returns. Needs Polymarket, out of scope. Order imbalance on Kalshi alone is a distinct, testable idea but our research found no tape rule for the 15 minute outcome.
Not found: any paper measuring market-making returns net of adverse selection, and any time-of-day effect study for event contracts.

## Top 5 by (distinct, testable now, effect size)
1. Maker side vs taker side, by category and by price (claim 1): about 1.1% a trade gap, more outside finance. Public tape. Distinct from our rule because it is about order role. Caveat: our bot is a taker in the 88c to 97c band, so this predicts our cost, not an edge.
2. Category and horizon calibration map (claim 3): per-category payoff distortion, sizes not verified. Testable on public settled markets.
3. Options-implied digital price vs Kalshi hourly ladder (claim 4): gap largest at low probability, long expiry (Polymarket 5.6 pts). Needs an outside vol feed.
4. Large-trade order imbalance forward return (claim 6): effect size not given in summary. Testable on Kalshi tape alone.
5. Longshot bias sold from the maker side at 1c to 10c (claim 2): longshot taker wins 0.43% vs maker 1.57% at 1c. Same family as favourite-late, listed only because it is the best sized claim; treat as rejected-adjacent.
Most testable on held data: claim 1 (taker-side tape, held recorder files R6/QUOTE2B).
